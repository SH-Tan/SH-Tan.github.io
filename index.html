Biography

I am a fourth-year Ph.D. student in the Department of Computer Science at Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute, advised by Prof. Radoslav Ivanov. My research focuses on efficient and explainable AI, particularly how neural models process information and how this understanding can improve their efficiency, interpretability, and reliability.

Before joining RPI, I received my master’s degree in Computer Technology from the University of Science and Technology of China, where I worked on perception and risk analysis for autonomous driving.

Research Interests

My work spans neural network analysis, model compression, and efficient inference. I study how information is represented and propagated within neural models, and how these insights can guide compression and knowledge transfer.

More recently, I have been exploring efficient and reliable inference for large language models (LLMs) in long-context, multi-turn, and long-horizon settings. This work examines how models should preserve and manage information over extended interactions, with particular attention to reliability challenges such as hallucination and distribution shifts.

My broader goal is to develop principled methods for understanding, compressing, and controlling modern neural models, making AI systems more efficient, interpretable, and dependable in real-world settings.
