I am a Ph.D. student in the Department of Computer Science at Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute, advised by Prof. Radoslav Ivanov. Before joining RPI, I received my master’s degree in Computer Technology from the University of Science and Technology of China in 2023, where I worked on autonomous-driving perception and risk assessment. I received my bachelor’s degree in Electrical Engineering and Automation from China University of Mining and Technology in 2020 and studied at the University of Wisconsin–Madison as an exchange student.

My research focuses on making neural networks and large language models more efficient and interpretable. I study how model structure and information flow can guide compression while preserving useful capabilities. My work includes graph-curvature methods for neural network pruning and robustness analysis, as well as distillation-aware pruning that considers both the damage caused by removing model components and what a teacher can help recover.

I am also exploring efficient inference in long-horizon settings, with a focus on key-value (KV) cache management. This direction asks how language models can retain useful context over extended interactions while working within limited memory budgets.

Current exploration · Efficient LLM inference

KV Cache Management for Long-Horizon Settings

I am exploring KV cache eviction and memory management for language models operating over long contexts and extended interactions. I am interested in how to decide which cached information to retain, when to evict it, and how to allocate limited memory as context grows. The goal is to balance memory use and inference efficiency with the ability to preserve information needed later in a task.

Current research · LLM compression

Distillation-Aware Pruning for LLMs

I develop pruning methods that account for what a teacher can repair during distillation. DAP jointly models immediate pruning damage and recoverability to design stronger compressed students.

Experiments on Qwen3-8B and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B show gains of up to 3.9 percentage points in average post-distillation performance over the strongest pruning baseline, under matched sparsity and training budgets.

Manuscript under review at ICLR 2027.

Explainable AI & neuro-symbolic learning

Understanding Neural Networks Through Graph Curvature

I represent neural networks as graphs and use Ricci curvature to interpret their information flow. This line of work connects model structure with two complementary questions: which connections can be pruned, and which bottlenecks are associated with fragile predictions?

Pruning: Post-Training Neural Network Pruning Using Graph Curvature

TMLR 2026

An interpretable post-training pruning method that identifies important information flows and structurally redundant connections using graph Ricci curvature. My work also includes GPU-accelerated curvature computation and extensions to transformer information-flow analysis.

PaperCode

Robustness: Analyzing Neural Network Robustness Using Graph Curvature

ICAA 2024

I introduce neural data graphs and neural Ricci curvature to identify structural bottlenecks associated with model robustness. Less robust predictions exhibit stronger bottleneck structures, providing a graph-based explanation of model vulnerability.

Autonomous driving · IET Intelligent Transport Systems 2024

RCP-RF: Road-Car-Pedestrian Risk Assessment

A driving risk potential field that jointly models road geometry, lane boundaries, vehicles, and pedestrians. This framework combines the surrounding traffic scene with relative-motion estimates to assess driving risk.
