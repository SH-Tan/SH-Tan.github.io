Current research · LLM compression

Distillation-Aware Pruning for LLMs

How should we compress a language model when it can learn from a teacher afterward? Pruning typically aims to minimize the immediate damage from removing weights, but the least damaged model is not necessarily the strongest student after distillation. I study how the teacher’s ability to recover that damage should guide what we prune.

Distillation-Aware Pruning (DAP) is a second-order framework that jointly models immediate pruning damage and recoverability under the intended teacher and distillation procedure. It estimates the damage that distillation cannot repair to guide the design of compressed students.

Experiments on Qwen3-8B and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B show gains of up to 3.9 percentage points in average post-distillation performance over the strongest pruning baseline, under matched sparsity and training budgets.

Manuscript under review at ICLR 2027.

Neural network analysis & explainable AI

Understanding Neural Networks Through Graph Curvature

Neural networks are often treated as black boxes, making it difficult to understand how their internal structure shapes their behavior. My work represents neural networks as directed graphs and uses Ollivier–Ricci curvature to analyze information flow and identify structural bottlenecks. These methods address two practical questions: which connections can be pruned, and which bottlenecks are associated with less robust predictions?

Interpretable Post-Training Pruning

TMLR 2026

An interpretable post-training pruning method that identifies important information flows and structurally redundant connections using graph Ricci curvature. I accelerated the shortest-path stage of curvature computation on GPU using dynamic programming and matrix-based operations. I also extended the framework to Llama 3 and IBM Granite by constructing graph representations of transformer information flow for model analysis and pruning.

Understanding Model Robustness

ICAA 2024

This work introduces neural data graphs and neural Ricci curvature to identify structural bottlenecks associated with model robustness. Less robust predictions exhibit stronger bottleneck structures, providing a graph-based explanation of model vulnerability.

Current exploration · Efficient LLM inference

KV Cache Management for Long-Horizon Settings

I am exploring key-value (KV) cache eviction and memory management for language models operating over long contexts and multi-turn interactions. This work examines which cached information to retain, when to evict it, and how to allocate limited memory as context grows. The goal is to improve inference efficiency while preserving information needed for reliable behavior over extended tasks.
